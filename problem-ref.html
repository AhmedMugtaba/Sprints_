>People often ask us why we're spending a cohort on Spatial AI. Here is the short version — what the problem is, why software has failed at it for decades, and why a small team has a real shot at it now.

The flat world

For most of computing history, software has lived in a flat world: text, tables, 2D images. Even the most advanced AI models, until recently, reasoned only about language and pixels.

But reality isn't flat. It is three‑dimensional, dynamic, and bound by physics. And nearly every trillion‑dollar industry — manufacturing, mining, construction, agriculture, logistics, energy — operates inside that world.

The bottleneck

The gap between what software understands and what reality demands has been the bottleneck for decades. Even sophisticated engineering organizations carry it:

  • CAD disconnected from reality.
  • Simulations disconnected from operations.
  • Robotics disconnected from environmental understanding.
  • AI disconnected from physical constraints.

The result is expensive site visits, manual surveying, downtime, safety risk, slow design iteration, reactive maintenance, and trillions of dollars of industrial data that nothing can act on.

For a long time, this looked like an unsolvable problem. Now it doesn't.

— Maien Hamed, partner

What just unlocked

Five enabling shifts crossed practical thresholds at almost the same moment:

  • 01
    Sensors became cheap and good. LiDAR, RGB‑D cameras, drones, IMUs, edge compute — the perception stack now ships in commodity hardware.
  • 02
    AI models became geometry‑aware. NeRFs, Gaussian splatting, foundation vision models, and multimodal transformers transformed scene understanding.
  • 03
    Robotics became commercially viable. Warehouses, mining, construction, agriculture, logistics, inspection — all automating at once, not one at a time.
  • 04
    Simulation and AI converged. Industrial simulation is now accelerated and approximated by ML surrogates and learned physical models.
  • 05
    Digital twin demand exploded. Organizations want continuously‑updated operational models, not static CAD files and disconnected dashboards.

The layer underneath

Spatial AI is the layer underneath all of this. The ability for machines and software to perceive 3D environments, localize within them, build persistent world models, reason about geometry and motion, predict physical interactions, and act in physical space.

This isn't a single market. It is the substrate of robotics, embodied AI, digital twins, geospatial intelligence, industrial modernization, and AI‑native engineering — at the same time. The transition is analogous to the shift from web software to software that understands and manipulates reality itself.

Why now, why this partner

The partner running this space — Maien Hamed — has spent 17 years building the pieces of this problem: simulation, ML infrastructure, perception, embedded systems, computational mechanics. Academia. Industrial R&D. Meta Reality Labs. Now his own company.

He is ready to bet the next decade on it. The two‑week sprint is how we co‑select the 3–5 people he bets it with.
